Where We Are

Paul makes a case. The case lands on you.

Paul arrives in Thessalonica with no building, no platform, and no institutional backing. He walks into a synagogue and does something deceptively simple: he opens the texts. For three Sabbaths he places the ancient prophecies alongside the life of Jesus and says — look at the match.

His case has three parts. The Messiah had to suffer. He had to die. And he rose from the dead. Two thousand years later, the case is still open. And it is not asking for your intellectual agreement. It is asking for your life.

I

Everyone treats resurrection like the last chapter.

The finale. The thing that happens after everything else is done — the dying, the waiting, the holding on. One day, when this life is over, you will be raised. That is the version most people carry.

Paul does not write it that way. Not even close.

“Since you have been raised with Christ, set your hearts on things above.”

Colossians 3:1 — past tense. Already done.

Not will be. Not might be if you endure long enough. Have been. The moment you came to Christ, the transaction was complete. Resurrection is not the chapter coming up. It is the chapter you are currently in.

Which means the grave has already lost its argument with you. Not eventually. Now. On whatever ordinary day you are standing in at this moment.

II

The power is not past tense.

Paul builds his case in Thessalonica from the Old Testament alone — Psalm 16, Isaiah 53, the suffering servant. He lays the evidence week by week until it becomes impossible to dismiss. And then, later, he writes to another church — not about the argument, but about what he personally wants:

“I want to know Christ — yes, to know the power of his resurrection.”

Philippians 3:10

Present tense. Ongoing. Available. Paul is not writing about a power that was active in the first century and has since been archived. He is describing something that can be known personally, right now — the same power that reversed death itself.

dunamis

Greek  ·  δυναμις  ·  power, might, the energy that produces results — root of our word “dynamite”

Not potential power. Not theoretical power. Operative, active, demonstrable power — the kind that produces results you can see. And Paul says this is what raised Jesus from the dead. Romans 8:11 tells you where it is now:

Romans 8:11 (NLT) “The Spirit of God, who raised Jesus from the dead, lives in you. And just as God raised Christ Jesus from the dead, he will give life to your mortal bodies by this same Spirit living within you.”

Not sitting in a museum. Not reserved for the first century. In you. Right now. The same measurable, historical, death-defeating force that cracked a Roman tomb open on a Sunday morning — resident in every person who belongs to Christ.

You are not running on your own power.
You are running on resurrection power.

III

What this actually changes.

A resurrected life does not mean a painless life. It does not mean your circumstances improve or the hard things stop being hard. It means your life has the same orientation as the resurrection — through death to life, through loss to gain, through the end of something to the beginning of something better.

Failure is not final.

The God who turned a crucifixion into a resurrection is not finished with your worst chapter. Failure is a moment in a story still being written — by someone who specialises in turning endings into beginnings.

Fear has lost its authority.

The thing you fear most has already been walked through by the one who walks with you. You are not heading toward an uncertain outcome. The ending of the story has already been secured.

Your old self has a death certificate.

Romans 6:4 — you were buried with him, and raised to walk in newness of life. You are not improving the old version. You are living a new one. Your past is not your identity. His resurrection is.

Monday looks different.

Resurrection power is not a Sunday phenomenon. It lives in you on a Tuesday at 3pm in a hard meeting, an exhausting season, the work nobody sees. You are not white-knuckling your way through life. You are resourced.

Ephesians 1:19–20 (NLT) “I also pray that you will understand the incredible greatness of God’s power for us who believe him. This is the same mighty power that raised Christ from the dead.”

IV

The shift from dead thinking to resurrected living.

Resurrection life is not automatic. You carry the power — but you can still choose to live as if you don’t. Most of us carry thought patterns that belong to the old life. Here are five common ones, and what the shift looks like when resurrection becomes present tense.

Am I Living Risen?

You cannot lose your resurrection life — it was given, not earned, and cannot be revoked. But there is a gap worth naming: the gap between having it and living from it. Five honest questions. Not a verdict — an invitation.

Question 1 of 5 0%

Question 01

When you fail — really fail — where does your mind go first?

“Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come.”

2 Corinthians 5:17

Shame and self-condemnation. I replay what went wrong and wonder what it says about who I am.

I know grace is real, but it takes me a while to get there. My first response is usually guilt.

To grace. I can acknowledge failure honestly without it becoming a verdict on my identity.

Question 02

Does your Monday feel connected to your Sunday — or are they two separate lives?

“The Spirit of God, who raised Jesus from the dead, lives in you.”

Romans 8:11

Completely separate. Faith is for Sunday. The rest of the week I am basically on my own.

Partly connected. I bring faith to the big moments but ordinary days feel pretty disconnected.

Connected. My faith is not a weekend category — it is the operating system underneath everything.

Question 03

When fear shows up — about the future, about what you might lose — how do you carry it?

“I am the Living One; I was dead, and now look, I am alive for ever and ever. And I hold the keys of death and Hades.”

Revelation 1:18

It consumes me. Fear about the future is one of my biggest ongoing struggles.

I manage it, but I am still doing a lot of the carrying myself.

I feel it, but I carry it differently. The outcome of my story is not uncertain — and that settles something.

Question 04

Are you waiting for something to change before you really start living?

“Since you have been raised with Christ, set your hearts on things above.”

Colossians 3:1

Yes. There are things that feel unresolved that I am waiting on before I can move forward.

Sometimes. I know the truth but I still find myself in a holding pattern more than I’d like.

No. I am living from what is already true, not toward something I am waiting to receive.

Question 05

Would the people closest to you say your life has a joy that does not depend on circumstances?

“Always be full of joy in the Lord. I say it again — rejoice!”

Philippians 4:4 (NLT)

Honestly, no. My joy is mostly circumstantial — it rises and falls with how things are going.

Sometimes. There are seasons of real joy, but it is not consistent enough for others to name first.

Yes. People around me would describe me as someone with a settled, genuine joy — even in hard seasons.

Discussion & Reflection

01

Paul says in Colossians 3 that you have been raised — past tense, already done. Does that feel true in how you actually live day to day? Where is the gap between the theology and the experience?

02

Romans 8:11 says the Spirit who raised Jesus from the dead lives in you. What would change about how you approached this week if you took that seriously — not as a comforting idea but as an operational reality?

03

Which of the five shifts is hardest for you right now — identity, failure, fear, Monday, or waiting? What would it look like to make that specific shift this week? Be concrete.

04

Family Prompt: Ask each person: if we genuinely believed resurrection power is alive in us right now — what would we do differently at home this week? Be specific. Then try one of those things before next Sunday.

05

Paul didn’t argue for the resurrection so people could believe a historical fact and move on. He argued for it because a historical fact became a present reality. Who in your world needs to hear that the life they have been waiting for has already started?

A Prayer for Those Still Living Like the Grave is Winning

Father, forgive us for treating the resurrection like a history lesson when you intended it to be a power source.

We confess that we have been living beneath what is already true. We have carried guilt that was cancelled, fear that has already been defeated, and identities that were buried when the old life died. We have been waiting for permission to arrive when the door has been open all along.

Today we choose to believe — not just that Jesus rose, but that we rose with him. That the same Spirit who raised him from the dead is alive in us right now. That Monday is not separate from Sunday. That failure is not our verdict. That the grave does not get the last word.

Teach us what it means to live resurrected. Not a better version of the old life — a genuinely new one. Starting today. Starting now.

In the name of Jesus — who is not dead, and neither are we. Amen.
