The Road Less Travelled

"He set his face to go to Jerusalem." — the servant walks the same road as his Master, all the way to the chains.

The hub for the Acts 21 series. Three postures of a life bent into the shape of the cross — surrender, accommodation, arrest — where the passion echoes peak, and then, on purpose, break.

The Turn in the Road

The pace itself is the message

Something shifts the moment Acts 21 opens. The place-names come fast — Cos, Rhodes, Patara, Tyre, Ptolemais, Caesarea, Jerusalem — the time-stamps are clipped, and Luke's eyewitness "we" is back on the boat. It reads like a travel diary because it is one. And Luke is doing it on purpose: he gave Jesus a long, deliberate journey up to Jerusalem in his Gospel — "he set his face to go to Jerusalem" (Luke 9:51) — and now he gives Paul one.

In Acts 19 Paul built; in Acts 20 he became like Christ as he went; here in Acts 21 he walks that likeness all the way in — a marked man sailing knowingly toward the chains, warned by the Spirit at every port. The road is narrowing toward the cost — the same road his Master walked. This is where the whole imitatio stops being a theme and becomes a journey with a mob at the end of it.

Where the rhyme breaks

A rhyme, not a photocopy

Agabus said Paul would be handed to the Gentiles — but in the event it is Gentiles who rescue him from his own people. Jesus' road ended on a cross; Paul's runs on — carried alive out of the mob, toward Rome. Paul is spared where the Master was slain — because the Master's cross needed no sequel. And precisely because it needed none, the story keeps moving: out of Jerusalem, down the centuries, all the way into this room.

The Invitation

Three postures, and not one of them is natural to us — hands open on a beach, freedom kneeling low, chains held up like a pulpit. Every one of them is caught from the One who walked the road first. Watch where Paul's feet are pointed this month, and ask where your own are.

The servant walks the Master's road — and even the goodbye kneels where Gethsemane knelt.
