Acts 17:5–9 · New Living Translation · Primary Evidence

"But some of the Jews were jealous, so they gathered some troublemakers from the marketplace to form a mob and start a riot. They attacked the home of Jason, searching for Paul and Silas so they could drag them out to the crowd. Not finding them there, they dragged out Jason and some of the other believers instead and took them before the city council.

"Paul and Silas have caused trouble all over the world," they shouted, "and now they are here disturbing our city, too. And Jason has welcomed them into his home. They are all guilty of treason against Caesar, for they profess allegiance to another king, named Jesus."

The people of the city, as well as the city council, were thrown into turmoil by these reports. So the officials forced Jason and the other believers to post bond, and then released them."

The Big Idea · Case Summary

The charges against Paul were meant as an indictment. Every accusation is theologically true. The prosecution accidentally made the case for the defence — and Luke knew exactly what he was doing when he wrote it down.

The question is not whether the early church was guilty. They were.
The question is whether the church today still is.

Investigator's Context Note

We are in Acts 17. Paul and Silas have been supernaturally released from a Philippian prison, crossed the Via Egnatia through Amphipolis and Apollonia, and arrived in Thessalonica — capital of Macedonia. For three Sabbaths Paul reasoned from the Scriptures that Jesus is the Messiah. Some were persuaded. Others were not. And when the city's Jewish leaders could not win the argument, they started a riot instead. The charges they bring are not slander. They are testimony.

Watch the foundation message · Acts 17:5–9

Count I of IV · Filed by: Thessalonian ComplainantsACTS 17:6
The Charge

"These men who have turned the world upside down have come here also."

Guilty
Theological Assessment

The mob meant this as an accusation. It is the most accurate job description of the apostles in the entire book of Acts. The Gospel has never been designed to make the existing order comfortable. It is not a coping mechanism. It is a reordering agent.

Investigator's Note · Greek Term
anastatoō (ἀναστατόω)

To unsettle, to drive out, to displace from one's homeland. The same verb appears in Galatians 5:12 and Acts 21:38 — carrying the weight of regime disruption, not mere controversy. It is the experience of a refugee: pulled from the familiar and dropped somewhere disorienting. Luke chose this word deliberately. That is exactly what genuine conversion is. You don't get to stay in the country you came from.

Exhibit A · Matthew 10:34

"Don't imagine that I came to bring peace to the earth! I came not to bring peace, but a sword."

Note on the Sword

The Greek word is machaira — a short cutting blade, not a battle sword. The same word appears in Hebrews 4:12 for the Word of God that divides soul and spirit. Jesus is not describing violence. He is describing the division of loyalties the Gospel produces wherever it enters. The sword does not create the division — it reveals it.

Application · Self-Examination

There is a version of Christianity that has fully assimilated. It does not unsettle anyone. It fits neatly inside the empire's categories — palatable, manageable, domesticated. The Thessalonians were panicking because something genuinely disruptive had entered their city.

Is the Christianity you are practising unsettling to anything?
Is it unsettling you?

Has it cost you a relationship? A financial decision? A version of yourself you were comfortable being? If the honest answer is not much — the Gospel may be functioning more as a comfort than a machaira.

Classified Finding · A Christianity that does not unsettle anything, that fits neatly inside the empire's categories, that would never cause a riot — is it the same thing Paul was dragged through the streets for?
Count II of IV · Filed by: Thessalonian ComplainantsACTS 17:7a
The Charge

"Jason has received them into his house."

Guilty
Theological Assessment

They did not charge Paul for preaching in the synagogue. That was legally protected — Judaism was a religio licita, a permitted religion. They could not touch him for that. So they went after the host. The crime was hospitality. A door opened. A table set. A movement housed.

Investigator's Note · The Oikos Pattern

The oikos — the household — was the operational base of the early church. An institution can be shut down. A home is everywhere. Every time Acts records the church growing, it grows outward from a household: Cornelius's house (Acts 10), Lydia's house (Acts 16), Jason's house (Acts 17), Priscilla and Aquila's house (Romans 16:5).

Exhibit B · Romans 16:5

"Also give my greetings to the church that meets in their home."

Application · The Living Room

We are not home-centric because it is trendy or convenient. We are home-centric because the oikos is the architecture God chose for the expansion of His Kingdom. The early church had no buildings, no clergy class, no institutional infrastructure. It had homes.

Your living room is not a substitute for the "real" church.
Your living room IS the church.

The charge against Jason is a compliment. The home is not a stepping stone to a building. It is the destination.

Filed Note · Jason didn't go looking for disruption. He just opened his door. That single act of hospitality cost him a court appearance, a financial bond, and almost certainly his social standing. The unsettling didn't come from trying to be radical — it came from one concrete, costly choice to align himself with something bigger than his own comfort.
Count III of IV · Filed by: Thessalonian ComplainantsACTS 17:7b
The Charge

"Acting contrary to the decrees of Caesar."

Guilty
Theological Assessment

The Pax Romana came with a theological claim: Caesar is Lord. Caesar is the son of god. Caesar brings peace. Every title the church gave Jesus — Kyrios, Soter, Divi Filius — was already taken. Already assigned. Already enforced with imperial power. To say "Jesus is Lord" was not a bumper sticker. It was sedition.

These were not metaphors. They were inscribed on coins, temples, and public monuments. When Paul preached "Jesus is Lord," every Roman citizen in the room heard the direct counter-claim.

Modern Decrees of Caesar · Filed for Examination
Decree 01 · Productivity as Identity
Your worth is measured by your output.

Counter-claim: You are God's poiēma — His masterpiece — before you do anything for Him. (Eph 2:10)

Decree 02 · Upward Mobility
Progress means accumulation. More is always the direction.

Counter-claim: The Kingdom consistently moves toward the margins, not away from them.

Decree 03 · Comfort as the Goal
The good life is the frictionless life.

Counter-claim: Jesus goes through Gethsemane, not around it. The cross is not a detour — it is the mission.

Decree 04 · The Algorithm
Visibility is validity. Engagement is truth.

Counter-claim: Paul didn't optimise his message. He reasoned from the Scriptures. That is not a scalable content strategy. It is faithfulness.

Decree 05 · The Autonomous Self
You are the final authority on your own life.

Counter-claim: "There is another King — one Jesus." (Acts 17:7)

Application

If your faith is not occasionally acting contrary to something — if it fits too neatly inside every system that makes demands on your time, money, and identity — you may have confused the Kingdom of God with the kingdom you are already living in.

Count IV of IV · Filed by: Thessalonian ComplainantsACTS 17:7c
The Charge

"There is another king — one Jesus."

Guilty
Theological Assessment

Five words. The most compressed, most accurate summary of Christian theology in the entire book of Acts. And it came from the mouth of the opposition. Notice what they do not deny: they do not say Jesus is dead. They do not dispute the resurrection. They call him a rival king — which means they concede he exists, he has followers, and his movement is real enough to threaten the existing order.

Investigator's Note · Basileus vs. Kyrios

The word used here is basileus — king, in the full political sense. Not a spiritual authority but a ruling monarch. This is the same charge brought before Pilate in John 19:12. In the ancient Near East, King of Kings was the title of whichever empire had conquered all the others — Babylon, Persia, Rome. When Revelation 19:16 places that title on the robe of the returning Christ — at his thigh, where the sword rests — every empire that ever carried it is being told: you were placeholders. He is the real one.

Investigator's Note · The Rightful King

"Rival" is the wrong word. In Genesis 3, humanity abandoned the viceregency God gave them. Into that vacancy, an occupying power moved in — Paul calls him "the god of this age" (2 Cor 4:4). He holds the territory but not the title deed. The cross was the eviction notice. The resurrection was the new locks on the door. When Jesus declares in Matthew 28:18 — "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me" — He is not receiving something new. He is reasserting what was always true. Jesus is not the challenger. He is the rightful King reclaiming what was always His.

Exhibit C · Revelation 19:16

"On his robe at his thigh was written this title: King of all kings and Lord of all lords."

Application · The Operative Question

The title King of Kings doesn't just exalt Jesus. It relativizes everything else. Every other king in our life has a subordinate position — the king of our finances, our career, our comfort, our reputation, our political allegiance. All of them answer to Him. All of them can be overruled by Him.

The question is not whether He's King of Kings. He is.
The question is whether you're living like it.

The prophetic question this passage puts to the Church is not historical.

It is present tense.
