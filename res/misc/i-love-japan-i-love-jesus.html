Section 1

Naming & identity

Don "What about calling this 'I Love ___ and Jesus'? I Love Saitama and Jesus, etc."

Don, we think your instinct here feels right — warm, local, disarming. "I Love Saitama and Jesus" doesn't sound like church to someone who's never been, which is exactly the point. The simplicity of it is appealing, and it works naturally as a social media handle or hashtag, with the prefecture slot functioning as a built-in localisation mechanism.

Section 2

Format & framing

Don "Perhaps a shorter internet weekly presentation would be easier — perhaps 15 minutes. I wonder if it would be good to not call it a worship service."

Not calling it a worship service might be the single most important framing decision we make. The moment you use that phrase, a script activates — in the minds of the people producing it and the people watching it. It shapes what gets included, what order things go in, what "success" looks like. A worship service has to be a certain thing. This doesn't.

Calling it something else — even just the name itself — keeps it open. Warm, local, informal, and still carrying genuine spiritual weight without needing to perform the shape of a service. The 15-minute format reinforces this. It's too short to be a service, which is a feature not a bug.

Section 3

Sustainability & getting started

Don "Pastors are busy and might not want an additional ministry. Could we produce a package each prefecture could use and then add the message from a pastor?"

Sustainability and time will be an ongoing challenge — and we should be honest about that upfront rather than minimise it. Pastors won't engage unless they can genuinely see the benefit, and any model that feels like another demand on an already stretched person will quietly die.

The package idea addresses this directly. If the structure is already built and the pastor's contribution is one short recorded message — filmed when it suits them, no live commitment required — it stops feeling like a new ministry and starts feeling like a slot they can fill. That's a very different ask.

A good early goal is to find even one or two pastors willing to try it as a proof of concept. Not a programme, not a launch — just a few real attempts to see what works.

We're from Melbourne (and you know we've been to Japan on missions), so we'd love to produce the starter ones with you. Don, it would be wonderful if you could be part of doing a few proof of concepts with us — sourcing a testimony, experimenting with local flavour, seeing what it actually feels like to make one of these.

Let's just produce some trial starters. Don, we'd really like to see this through with you. If this is one way The Living Room can pursue missions in Japan, we'd be honoured to work this through.

This ties directly into the videography question. What we've found is that the less produced something feels, the more organic the response tends to be. There's a level of quality that matters — audio especially — but heavy production values can work against you. It signals "broadcast" when what you want is "conversation."

Section 4

Videography & cost

Don "What extra equipment and cost would be needed? Would a videographer or multiple videographers be good?"

Equipment doesn't need to be uniform across prefectures. The minimum viable kit above handles the weekly content. A shared videographer who travels between prefectures periodically could produce higher-quality feature pieces — slower to make, but longer-lasting assets that celebrate each prefecture's culture and landscape.

The unproduced feeling is actually an asset. What we've observed is that organic, real, slightly imperfect content travels further and lands more genuinely than polished broadcast-style production. The goal is a conversation, not a show.

Section 5

The webpage as the episode

The clips don't need to be stitched together into one file. The webpage itself is the container — it plays the clips in sequence, guiding the viewer from local content through to testimony, pastor's message, and a closing song. Each segment is its own file. Swappable, reusable, updatable without touching the others.

Section 6

The content bank

Every proof of concept you produce isn't just one episode — it's assets going into a bank. The first five testimonies recorded are the beginning of a library that eventually feeds an automated system. You're not wasting effort on experiments; you're building infrastructure from day one.

The tagging discipline you put in at the proof of concept stage is what enables everything that comes later. Tag well now and the system almost builds itself over time.

How each clip gets tagged

Place

Saitama Tokyo Hokkaido Okinawa Tohoku Kyoto

Theme

belonging hope family new beginnings grief seasons

Content type

local-footage testimony pastor-message closing-song

One clip carries multiple tags. A testimony filmed in Saitama about belonging gets tagged: Saitama + testimony + belonging. It can appear on a Saitama page or in a "belonging" themed week anywhere.

Section 7

Dynamic assembly

Once the tagging is in place, a page can build itself. When someone visits ilovejapan.com/saitama, the system queries the bank for the latest tagged clips in each slot and assembles the page — no human intervention needed.

Page request

Visitor loads /saitama

System knows: place = Saitama, week = current

↓

Slot 1 — local footage

Find: type=local-footage AND place=Saitama

Returns latest, or rotates through available clips

↓

Slot 2 — testimony

Find: type=testimony AND (place=Saitama OR theme=this-week)

Returns most recent unused testimony for this visitor

↓

Slot 3 — pastor message

Find: type=pastor-message AND (place=Saitama OR theme=this-week)

Could be from any pastor in the bank

↓

Page assembles

Four clips, in sequence, on a clean page

No human intervention needed once clips are in the bank

The first version of this could run on something as simple as a Google Sheet as the database, YouTube for hosting, and a basic webpage that reads from the sheet. No custom infrastructure needed to start. You scale the technology as the content bank grows.
